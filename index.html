Graphic Design

Past • Present • Future

Overview

Graphic design has been around for thousands of years and can be traced back to ancient civilizations such as Egypt, where hieroglyphics were used as a form of visual communication. Over time, the tools used in design have evolved from simple materials to advanced digital technology. This website explores the transition from ancient tools to modern applications and considers how future technologies may continue to transform the field of graphic design.

Part A: Existing Technology

Ancient Tools

Ancient designers relied on tools such as clay tablets and papyrus scrolls. Clay tablets were made from soft clay and written on using a stylus, mainly for recording laws, messages, and important information. Papyrus scrolls were an early form of paper created from the papyrus plant and written on with ink and reed pens. These tools were essential for preserving communication and early design.

Modern Apps

Today, designers use digital tools like PicMonkey, GIMP, and Visme. PicMonkey is an easy to use design platform that offers tutorials and tools for both beginners and experienced users. GIMP provides advanced features such as layers, filters, and plug-ins, making it similar to professional software like Photoshop. Visme is a cloud-based tool used to create presentations, graphics, and animations with templates and collaboration features.

Devices

Traditional Devices

Traditional devices such as lightboxes and drafting tables played an important role in design before the digital era. A lightbox is a flat surface with a bright light that allows artists to trace designs and create clean lines. Drafting tables are large angled surfaces that help artists sketch layouts accurately and comfortably.

Modern Devices

Modern devices include tools such as sound tablets, flatbed scanners, and external SSDs. Sound tablets provide feedback that mimics drawing on paper, helping artists control strokes more naturally. Flatbed scanners allow physical artwork to be digitized while preserving detail and texture. External SSDs provide fast storage and allow designers to save and transfer large files efficiently.

Part B: Future Technology

Holographic Canvas

The holographic canvas is a future concept that could allow designers to create three-dimensional designs in real time. It would be able to scan drawings and project them into 3D,automatically fix proportions,and simulate materials such as watercolor,charcoal, or oil pastel. It could also generate textures like metal,fabric,or wool and replace traditional tools like keyboards with gesture based controls using smart gloves.

Dream Printer

The dream printer is another future technology that could instantly print designs directly from software. It could print with special materials such as invisible ink visible under UV light while using less ink overall. This device would improve efficiency and expand creative possibilities for designers.
