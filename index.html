Graphic Design

Past • Present • Future

Overview

Graphic design has been around for thousands of years, even being traced back to ancient Egypt where they used hieroglyphics. So how did a transition happen with the tools from ancient times to present days? This site will explore the old tools, present, and possibility of the future.

Part A: Existing Technology

Ancient Tools

Clay Tablets:
made from soft clay
people wrote on them using a sharp stylus
used for keeping records, messages, and laws
Papyrus Scrolls:
early type of paper made from the papyrus plant
written on with ink and reed pens
used for letters, books, and important documents

Hardware / Devices

Old Devices:

Lightbox:
a flat box with a bright light inside
artists place paper on top to trace designs
used for animation, lettering, and clean line work

Drafting Table:
large angled table for drawing
helps artists sketch layouts and designs accurately
used before digital drawing tablets

Present Devices:

Device 1: Sound Tablet
a digital drawing tablet that makes vibration or sound feedback
mimics the feeling of paper
helps artists get a traditional feel
makes it easier to control strokes and pressure
creates a bridge between traditional and digital art

Device 2: Flatbed Scanner
a glass surface used to scan and digitize anything on paper
preserves details and textures of artwork
used to create digital copies
helps combine traditional and digital techniques
keeps artwork high quality

Device 3: External SSD
portable storage device
stores large design files
expands storage
transfers projects quickly
loads and saves files faster

Part B: Future Technology

Holographic Canvas

can project 3D versions of what you draw
can scan drawings and project them into 3D
fixes proportions automatically
simulates materials like watercolor, charcoal, or oil pastel
generates materials like metal, fabric, or wool
replaces keyboard and mouse with a smart glove

Dream Printer

syncs with design programs instantly
can print anything
prints invisible ink seen under UV or blue light
uses less ink than other printers
